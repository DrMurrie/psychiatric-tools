Step 4 of 7 — Medications

Current Medications

Tick all medication classes present. Selected items generate specific alerts in the output.

High — active management required
LithiumHIGH — toxicity risk
GI-induced vomiting/diarrhoea → dehydration → reduced renal clearance → toxicity. Three published cases of semaglutide-precipitated lithium toxicity. Levels: baseline, 2wks, post-each escalation. Semaglutide preferred. Extend escalation intervals to 6–8wks. ≥2L/day hydration.
Insulin / sulfonylureaHIGH — hypoglycaemia
Additive glucose lowering. Reduce insulin 20–30% at initiation; suspend or reduce sulfonylurea. Tirzepatide: greater glycaemic potency — more aggressive reduction needed. Early BGL review; hypoglycaemia action plan provided.
Oral contraceptive pill (OCP)HIGH — tirzepatide only
Tirzepatide delays gastric emptying → reduced OCP absorption → contraceptive failure risk. Switch to non-oral or add barrier method for 4wks after initiation and each escalation (TGA Nov 2025). Semaglutide: no clinically significant OCP interaction.
Moderate — monitor and adjust
ClozapineMODERATE — level monitoring
Significant weight loss alters volume of distribution → possible increased clozapine plasma levels. Check level: baseline, 4wks, 3 months. COaST RCT (Siskind et al., Lancet Psychiatry 2025) supports semaglutide first-line.
Anticholinergic antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine, chlorpromazine, clozapine)MODERATE — additive GI slowing
Anticholinergic antipsychotics slow gastric emptying independently. GLP-1 RAs also delay gastric emptying — additive effect increases constipation, nausea, and delayed oral drug absorption. Monitor bowel frequency; prophylactic stool softener considered.
Antihistaminergic psychotropics (mirtazapine, quetiapine, olanzapine, TCAs)MODERATE — reduced weight loss efficacy
H1-antihistamine antagonism increases appetite and carbohydrate craving — may blunt GLP-1-mediated satiety and reduce weight loss response. Consider switch to weight-neutral agent if clinically appropriate. Counsel patient on possible attenuated response.
Stimulants (methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine, dexamfetamine)MODERATE — absorption + additive appetite suppression
Delayed gastric emptying may alter Tmax and absorption of IR formulations. Additive appetite suppression increases risk of inadequate nutritional intake. Consider switch to long-acting formulation. Monitor nutritional status and weight weekly initially.
ValproateMODERATE — level shift with weight loss
Highly protein-bound; significant weight loss alters free fraction and volume of distribution. Check total and free valproate at 3 and 6 months. Check mid-cycle if clinical change occurs.
Warfarin / narrow therapeutic index drugs (digoxin, cyclosporine, tacrolimus)MODERATE — altered absorption
GE delay alters absorption rate and Tmax of narrow TI oral drugs. INR weekly × 4 weeks and post-each escalation. Drug levels (digoxin, cyclosporine) at initiation and each escalation. Liaise with prescribing specialist.
Watch — generally compatible, monitor
SSRIs / SNRIsWATCH
No major PK interaction. Weight-gain SSRIs (paroxetine, escitalopram) may partially offset benefit. Monitor appetite, mood, weight trend.
MetforminWATCH
Standard compatible combination in T2DM. Review eGFR — hold if eGFR <30. Consider ceasing if T2DM achieves remission on GLP-1 RA.
Naltrexone / acamprosate / buprenorphineWATCH
No direct PK interaction. Both act on reward circuitry — monitor for cumulative appetite and motivational suppression. Do not co-prescribe Contrave (naltrexone/bupropion) with GLP-1 RA without specialist review.
Antihypertensives (ACE-I, ARB, beta-blocker, CCB)WATCH
Weight loss reduces BP — existing antihypertensives may become excessive → postural hypotension. Review dose at 3 and 6 months. Particularly important in elderly.
Benzodiazepines / z-drugsWATCH
Reduced GI motility + sedation increases aspiration risk if vomiting occurs. Relevant for ECT, procedures, anaesthesia. No direct PK interaction.

Titration Protocol

Titration & Nutritional Guidance

Titration rules
  • Same day each week; rotate injection sites
  • Hold escalation if ≥2 nausea/vomiting episodes/week — stay 4 more weeks then retry
  • Extend to 6–8wks: lithium patients, elderly, GI sensitivity
  • Rapid weight loss (>1 kg/week): hold, increase protein, consider temporary dose reduction
  • Target: ≥5% weight loss at 6 months at maximum tolerated dose
Nutritional
  • Protein ≥1.2–1.5 g/kg/day throughout treatment
  • Daily multivitamin + mineral supplement
  • Vitamin D + calcium: elderly, long-term antipsychotics
  • ≥2 L water/day — critical if on lithium, sulfonylurea, or diuretics
  • Dietitian referral at initiation (mandatory: elderly, ASD/ID, eating disorders, rapid weight loss)
  • Exercise physiologist referral — resistance training to mitigate sarcopenia
